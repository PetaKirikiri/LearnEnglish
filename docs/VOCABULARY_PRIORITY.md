# Vocabulary priority review — 10 November 2026

The previous order was too influenced by the available exercise objects and illustrations. An object's ability to fill a verb frame is not evidence that it belongs before basic people or family vocabulary.

## Sources consulted

- [Oxford 3000: selection criteria](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/about/oxford3000): frequency, use across contexts, and practical learner relevance all matter.
- [Oxford 3000 by CEFR level](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/external/pdf/wordlists/oxford-3000-5000/The_Oxford_3000_by_CEFR_level.pdf): basic people/family vocabulary and run are A1; hold is A2. The list is alphabetical within levels, not a recommended item-by-item teaching order. Book and apple are also A1: their presence does not justify putting them before people.
- [British Council A1–A2 vocabulary](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/vocabulary/a1-a2): beginner topics cover actions, routines, everyday objects, homes, food/drink, people, clothes, colours and other useful situations. This supports broad everyday coverage rather than a long sequence of object illustrations.
- [Cambridge Pre A1 Starters preparation](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/qualifications/young-learners/paper/starters/preparation/): picture-based vocabulary resources provide an additional beginner reference, but this children's exam syllabus is not used as an adult student's exact priority order.

## Applied decisions

The shared noun order begins man, woman, boy, girl, mother, father, son and daughter, then practical possessions and needs. Nouns have two independent pools: two Current subjects (people and animals) and two Current objects (things without agency), each with its own queued replacement. Verbs have three Current transitive and three Current intransitive uses with independent queues. Other word types have at most four unfinished Current words and one queued replacement. A learnt word leaves its slot immediately, so an earlier gap stays Current while another slot can refill. Food-example nouns no longer determine the starting pool. Running and walking are available early; holding is later. This exact sequence is our teaching judgement informed by the sources and the user's priorities, not a published frequency ranking.

The action course can review previously taught bare actions at Level 6. It no longer needs to introduce holding or a food noun merely to produce a full set. New nouns and verbs still require the same independent listening evidence, and all entry levels use the same learner inventory. Historical scores backfill fading, but exposure alone does not prove a word. Verified words remain available for context and scheduled review; earlier unverified gaps stay in the Current pool.

The review page remains lists only. It displays the implemented curriculum, not a claim to cover every essential beginner word.

## Coverage still to add

The current inventory remains incomplete. Brother, sister, friend, child, person, family, home, room, food, rice, bread, milk, money and car are clear candidates for the next content expansion. Everyday actions such as going, coming, sitting, standing, working, looking and buying also need appropriate taught constructions and pictures. Those omissions should drive content work; the existence of illustrations for less useful objects must not set their priority.

Pronoun subjects enter compatible sentence structures after two distinct noun families are Learnt, counting subjects and objects together. Fading or exposure alone does not unlock them; singular and plural forms count as one family. Existing proved pronouns remain available for established learners. Once unlocked, every third question opportunity prioritizes a Current pronoun when the structure and known vocabulary support it; noun practice and due reviews continue. The audio forecast uses the same threshold.

Subject and object compatibility is reviewed separately in `actionSemantics.ts`. The boy, girl, son and daughter entries depict children and cannot drive; that restriction applies to composition, alternatives and the teacher picker. Explicit affordances distinguish portable, openable, washable, cleanable and readable nouns. Cleaning covers furniture, appliances and appropriate places; it is not a blanket fallback for every noun. The diagnostic reads these same rules, and all nouns currently in the hierarchy have at least one supported action.

The expansion adds using, carrying, finding, buying, closing, wearing, putting on, taking off, riding, catching, getting on, helping, watching, teaching, sitting, standing, waiting, arriving and leaving. New entries follow the existing hierarchy without increasing the three-per-verb-group Current limit. Reviewed object affordances and child/transport restrictions apply to both correct choices and distractors. All nineteen new actions and cleaning have matching transparent 3D icons in public/classroom/icons/actions/expanded; prompt provenance is in docs/artwork/expanded-verbs.json.

Adjectives use the reviewed noun whitelist in adjectiveSemantics.ts across both Subjects and Objects. People take height descriptions (tall/short), never colour or temperature labels. Manufactured objects can take reviewed colours and sizes; fruit colours are constrained (red/green apple, green/yellow banana), and hot/cold applies to water and sandwiches. Both alternatives must fit their noun. Subject and object descriptions remain separate cards inside their own yellow noun groups, and may coexist in one sentence. Matching adjectives are inspectable below each noun in the diagnostic.

Action practice preserves its sentence construction whenever compatible questions exist. The shared transitive sequence starts with holding, opening and using so the initial phone/bag object group can participate in logical sentences. Noun-only prerequisite recall is used only when the chosen construction has no compatible questions. Scores and listening evidence stay account-wide across lesson changes.
