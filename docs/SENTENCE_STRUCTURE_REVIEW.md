## The mistake

We used a change in meaning, a curriculum topic, or a highlighted word as evidence of a new sentence structure. Those are not interchangeable.

“The bird ate the seed”, “Only the bird ate the seed”, and “The bird ate only the seed” differ in focus and meaning, but retain the same basic active clause. The last two belong as optional comparisons inside that pattern, not new top-level structures. Removing their separate section was appropriate; deleting useful modifier teaching everywhere would not be.

In these examples, *only* is a focusing adverb. It is not generally true that modifiers cannot affect syntax: “Only then did the bird eat” also introduces inversion. Count the constructional change, not the presence of the word *only*.

## Authoring standard

Teach a small number of reusable construction families with visible variations, using the same bird/seed world. Keep three categories separate:

1. **Pattern:** how participants, verbs, complements and clauses are assembled; how auxiliaries establish time, aspect, polarity or a question.
2. **Variation:** vocabulary, reference, quantity, agreement, degree, focus or a different modal meaning inside an already established pattern.
3. **Recap/composition:** revisit a known pattern or combine known constructions. Useful practice, but not automatically another new structure.

Before adding a new pattern, ask:

- What existing pattern is this closest to?
- What changes besides the words filling its positions?
- Does the learner need a different arrangement, helper/verb-form combination, complement, participant relationship or clause relationship?
- Can the distinction be shown as a variation under the existing pattern?
- Has this exact exchange already appeared? If so, why repeat it here?

A different communicative purpose is not, by itself, a new syntactic pattern. Conversely, identical broad subject–verb–object order does not make “eats”, “is eating” and “has eaten” interchangeable: their auxiliary/verb constructions are precisely part of what this course must expose.

These are course-design groupings, not a claim that linguistics has one universal count of English structures. Do not inflate counts to meet a target.

## The conditional example stays

**Would the bird be hungry now if it had eaten the seed?**

This combines a question about an imagined present result with a past-perfect condition. Its reusable frame is:

`Would + subject + base-form predicate + if + subject + had + past participle?`

Here, *now* makes the present result explicit; the construction is not new merely because *now* was added. The past condition and present hypothetical result are what distinguish this mixed conditional from the past-result version using *would have been*. Preserve the contrast between those two.

## Concrete duplication

Six exact English question/answer exchanges recur across sections (ignoring emphasis markup):

- First actions / completion: “What is the bird doing?” → “The bird is eating the seed.”
- First negatives / verb shapes: “Is the bird eating the seed?” → “The bird is eating the seed.”
- Be forms / past state: “Is the bird hungry?” → “The bird is hungry.”
- Location / indirect questions: “Where is the bird?” → “The bird is beside the seed.”
- Modals / verb shapes: “Can the bird eat the seed?” → “The bird can eat the seed.”
- Passive / relative clauses: “What did the bird do?” → “The bird ate the seed.”

Some are useful contrast anchors. Keep them where needed, but distinguish repeated examples from unique constructions. Do not mechanically delete the baseline from a useful comparison.

## Implementation recommendation

1. Separate the internal pattern-family identity from curriculum topic IDs; one topic need not produce a new structure card.
2. Assign examples to a family and mark their internal purpose: new construction, variation, contrast anchor or synthesis.
3. Combine the clear duplicate families and move reference/quantity/focus choices inside their relevant patterns.
4. Preserve the familiar opening, question → answer layout, illustrations and aligned Thai meanings. Do not add classification badges or explanatory scaffolding to the learner UI.
5. Review the resulting families before renumbering. Report section, example and pattern counts separately.
