# Reading question language

Use the photographed textbook as the guide to the learner's language level. We
have the syllabus and passages, not the actual test questions; do not claim these
are exact exam questions or a validated readiness measure.

Questions should test understanding of the passage without adding an unrelated
test of academic English. Prefer direct What, Where, Who, When, How many, How,
and Why questions. Keep choices short, natural and supported by the visible text.

- Ask “Is the Drina River house beside the water?” instead of “Which description
  does the passage explicitly reject?”
- Ask “What do the steel columns do?” instead of “Why are the steel columns
  mentioned?”
- Ask “Which order is right?” instead of discussing a stated sequence.
- Teach negation, comparison, pronoun reference and some/many through concrete
  questions. Do not label these operations with academic terminology.
- Keep source passages intact. Thai explanations and individual word help remain
  available in their existing places; do not add full prompt translations.

## Vocabulary clues

Use a natural sentence that a learner can complete, such as “I wash the dishes
in the _____.” Avoid a definition awkwardly attached to a blank with a colon
(“this basin with a tap: the _____”). Definitions should use familiar language;
words such as “fixture”, “appliance” and “padded” add unnecessary difficulty.

Check the complete sentence against every offered choice after simplifying it.
Exclude alternatives that also fit a common situation, such as eating meals in
either a kitchen or dining room, watching TV in a bedroom or living room, or
looking through a glass door or window. Keep the same textbook vocabulary,
question IDs and practice/assessment split.
